## How rubber parts are made: the common steps

Every part in this guide starts the same way, whatever its final shape.

1. **Compounding.** Raw polymer is mixed with fillers, oils, curing agents and
   protective chemicals into a compound. Margo mixes its eight compounds
   in-house: NBR, EPDM, FKM, silicone, AEM, neoprene, natural rubber and SBR.
   The compound decides what the finished part can survive: oil, heat, water,
   ozone or wear.
2. **Tooling.** A steel mould, or for profiles a die, is cut to the part
   drawing. Margo cuts its own tools in-house.
3. **Shaping and curing.** Heat and pressure turn the soft compound into
   elastic rubber, a step called vulcanisation or curing. This is done either
   in a mould (moulding) or as a continuous length (extrusion).
4. **Post-cure and finishing.** Some compounds, such as silicone and FKM, are
   baked again in an oven to stabilise their properties. Excess rubber at the
   mould split line, called flash, is removed by cryogenic deflashing or by
   trimming.
5. **Inspection.** Parts are checked for size against ISO 3302-1 tolerance
   classes and for hardness, and the compound is tested for tensile strength,
   compression set and ageing.

### The three moulding methods

- **Compression moulding:** a measured piece of compound is placed in the open
  mould and the press closes on it. Suited to thick sections and larger parts
  such as pads, buffers, mounts and large gaskets.
- **Transfer moulding:** the compound is pushed from a chamber into a closed
  mould through small channels. Suited to parts with metal inserts and finer
  detail.
- **Injection moulding:** heated compound is injected into a closed mould.
  Suited to high volumes of smaller, precise parts.

### Extrusion

For profiles, cords and tubes, the compound is forced through a shaped die
and cured continuously as it comes out. The result is a length with the same
cross-section all the way along, which is then cut to size or joined into a
closed frame.

## O-rings

**What it is.** A ring of rubber with a round cross-section. It sits in a
groove and is squeezed between two parts, and that squeeze seals the joint.

**How it is made.** O-rings are moulded in multi-cavity moulds, many at a time,
then deflashed so the parting line does not leave a leak path. Large rings can
be made from extruded cord, cut and vulcanised at the joint. Margo moulds them
to AS568, ISO 3601, JIS B2401 and DIN 3771 sizes, or to your drawing.

**Why it is needed.** An O-ring is the simplest, cheapest way to seal a joint
against liquid or gas, and it works under pressure in both directions.

**Where it is used.**
- *Industry:* hydraulic and pneumatic cylinders, pumps, valves, fuel systems,
  refrigerant valves, compressors and pipe couplings.
- *Appliances:* water purifier filter housings and taps, pressure cooker
  safety valves, mixer and juicer jars, coffee machines, washing machine water
  inlets and gas stove fittings.

## Food-grade O-rings

**What it is.** An O-ring moulded in a compound chosen for contact with food
or drink, usually silicone or EPDM, often in white, blue or red so a broken
piece is easy to see.

**How it is made.** The same moulding process as any O-ring, in a compound
selected for the product, the temperature and the cleaning cycle. Margo moulds
food-grade O-rings on request. Margo holds no food-contact certificate for any
compound, so food-contact compliance stays with the equipment maker's approval.

**Why it is needed.** Standard black compounds can taint food or break down
under hot cleaning and steam. A food-grade compound is chosen so the seal
survives the cleaning cycle without contaminating the product.

**Where it is used.**
- *Industry:* dairy and brewery plant, bottling and filling lines, sanitary
  pipe couplings, food processing machinery and pharma process equipment.
- *Appliances:* water dispensers, coffee machines, juicers, blenders and
  kitchen storage lids.

## Static oil seals

**What it is.** A seal that keeps oil inside a housing where the parts do not
move against each other: a cover, a plug or a joint. It usually has a
rubber-covered outer diameter and no garter spring.

**How it is made.** Moulded in an oil-resistant compound, most often NBR, or
FKM or AEM where the oil runs hot.

**Why it is needed.** Covers and plugs on oil-filled housings need a seal that
grips the bore, does not shrink or swell in oil, and stays tight through heat
cycles for years.

**Where it is used.**
- *Industry:* gearbox and engine covers, compressor housings, hydraulic power
  packs, pump end covers.
- *Appliances:* refrigerator and AC compressors, and oil-filled motor
  housings.

## Metal bonded oil seals

**What it is.** A rotary oil seal with a steel case or insert bonded to the
rubber. The rubber lip presses on the turning shaft, held in place by a garter
spring, and keeps oil in and dirt out.

**How it is made.** The metal case is cleaned and prepared, coated with a
primer and a cover coat matched to the compound, and placed in the mould. The
rubber is moulded around it and bonded to it in the same cure, so there is no
glued joint to fail. The garter spring is fitted afterwards. Margo moulds them
to DIN 3760 and ISO 6194 profiles or to your drawing.

**Why it is needed.** The metal gives the seal a rigid, accurate press fit in
the housing, so it does not turn or walk out. The rubber lip does the sealing
on the moving shaft.

**Where it is used.**
- *Industry:* car, tractor and two-wheeler gearboxes and axles, electric
  motors, pumps, compressors and industrial gearboxes.
- *Appliances:* washing machine drum bearings, mixer grinder motors and
  pump sets.

## Rubber profiles

**What it is.** A continuous length of rubber with a fixed cross-section: a
bulb seal, a channel, a fin, a cord or a tube.

**How it is made.** Extruded through a die cut to the section drawing and
cured continuously, then cut to length or joined at the corners into a closed
frame. Sponge profiles are made by adding a blowing agent so the rubber foams
as it cures. Margo extrudes in EPDM, NBR, FKM, silicone and neoprene.

**Why it is needed.** Doors, windows and lids need a seal that runs all the
way round an edge, closes with a light push and springs back every time.

**Where it is used.**
- *Industry:* bus, truck, rail coach and tractor cab doors and windows,
  building glazing, electrical panels and enclosures, refrigerated vans.
- *Appliances:* refrigerator and freezer doors, oven and microwave door seals,
  dishwasher doors, display chillers and washing machine lids.

## Grommets

**What it is.** A rubber ring or sleeve that fits into a hole in a panel so a
cable, pipe or rod can pass through it.

**How it is made.** Moulded, with a groove that snaps into the panel hole.
Margo makes open, blind, multi-hole, oval and bulkhead grommets from 6 mm
panel hole upward, and to drawing above that.

**Why it is needed.** A sheet-metal edge cuts through a cable's insulation over
time, especially when the machine vibrates. A grommet protects the cable,
seals the hole against water and dust, and damps vibration.

**Where it is used.**
- *Industry:* vehicle wiring harnesses and firewalls, control panels,
  switchgear, tractors and machinery.
- *Appliances:* the back panels of washing machines, refrigerators and ACs
  where the power cord and pipes enter, and in fans, coolers and geysers.

## Rubber bushes

**What it is.** A cylinder of rubber, often bonded to a metal inner sleeve, that
sits between two parts that move slightly against each other.

**How it is made.** Moulded to the bore, outer diameter, length and hardness
required. In a bonded bush, the steel sleeve is primed and the rubber is
moulded and bonded to it in one cure.

**Why it is needed.** The rubber lets the joint flex and twist a little, while
absorbing vibration and noise that would otherwise travel through the
structure. It also needs no lubrication.

**Where it is used.**
- *Industry:* vehicle suspensions and shackles, engine and transmission
  mounts, tractor linkages, pump and motor mountings, and water-lubricated
  bearings in submersible pumps.
- *Appliances:* washing machine suspension and motor mounts, AC compressor
  mounts and cooler motors.

## Rubber cushion bushes

**What it is.** A rubber bush whose main job is to cushion shock rather than
locate a part. The best-known type sits on the pins of a pin-bush coupling
between a motor and the machine it drives.

**How it is made.** Moulded, with the hardness set to the torque and the shock
it has to carry.

**Why it is needed.** When a motor starts, the torque hits the coupling at
once. The cushion bush takes that shock, allows for small misalignment between
the two shafts and stops vibration passing from one machine to the other.

**Where it is used.**
- *Industry:* pin-bush and jaw couplings on pumps, compressors, gearboxes and
  conveyors; the centre bearing cushion on truck, bus and tractor propeller
  shafts.
- *Appliances and equipment:* domestic pump sets, generator sets and
  compressors.

## Rubber support pads

**What it is.** A solid block or shaped pad of rubber that a machine or unit
stands on.

**How it is made.** Compression moulded, usually in natural rubber, neoprene
or EPDM, sized to the weight the pad carries.

**Why it is needed.** A running machine shakes. A [rubber support pad](/products/pads-stoppers-caps/big-support-pad) carries the
weight, keeps the unit level, stops it walking across the floor and keeps
vibration and noise out of the floor or wall.

**Where it is used.**
- *Industry:* compressors, generator sets, pumps, machine tools, haul truck
  bodies, and vehicle [body mounts](/products/pads-stoppers-caps/bolero-support-pad) and cab mounts.
- *Appliances:* AC outdoor units on wall brackets, washing machine feet,
  refrigerator compressor mounts, inverter and battery stands.

## Diaphragms

**What it is.** A flexible rubber disc that moves back and forth to push a
fluid, or that separates two chambers and flexes when the pressure changes.

**How it is made.** Moulded flat, rolling or convoluted. For higher pressure,
layers of nylon, polyester or aramid fabric are bonded into the rubber during
the cure. Simple flat diaphragms can be die-cut from sheet with no mould
needed. The compound is chosen for the fluid the diaphragm touches on every
stroke.

**Why it is needed.** A diaphragm moves fluid or senses pressure without a
sliding seal, so nothing leaks past a piston and the fluid stays away from the
moving parts.

**Where it is used.**
- *Industry:* chemical dosing pumps, air-operated double-diaphragm pumps,
  weir valves, gas and pressure regulators, agricultural sprayers and brake
  chambers.
- *Appliances:* water purifier pumps and valves, washing machine pressure
  switches, LPG regulators and gas geyser valves.

## Rubber sealings: gaskets and seals

**What it is.** The general name for rubber parts that seal one surface
against another: flat gaskets, flange gaskets, lid and door seals, washers
and moulded seals of any shape.

**How it is made.** Moulded for shaped or thick parts, extruded and joined for
long perimeters, or cut from rubber sheet for flat gaskets. The compound
follows the medium: NBR for oil, EPDM for water and steam, silicone for heat,
FKM for chemicals.

**Why it is needed.** No two metal faces are perfectly flat. A rubber gasket
fills the gaps between them, so a joint stays tight under pressure, heat and
vibration.

**Where it is used.**
- *Industry:* pipe flanges, pump and valve bodies, engine and gearbox covers,
  EV battery packs, electrical enclosures and sanitary food pipework.
- *Appliances:* pressure cooker gaskets, refrigerator and oven doors, water
  heater and purifier fittings, and tap washers.

## Choosing the right compound

The same part can last for years or fail in weeks depending on the compound.
As a starting point:

| Compound | Best for | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NBR (nitrile) | Oil, fuel, hydraulic fluid | Weather, ozone, steam |
| EPDM | Water, steam, weather, brake fluid | Mineral oil and fuel |
| FKM (Viton) | Aggressive chemicals, fuels, high heat | Steam, cost-sensitive parts |
| Silicone (VMQ) | Very high and very low temperatures, food-contact grades on request | Wear and abrasion, rotating shafts |
| AEM (Vamac) | Hot oil in engine and transmission bays | Steam, fuel |
| Neoprene (CR) | Refrigerants, moderate oil, flame-retardant grades | Strong oils and fuels |
| Natural rubber | Damping, abrasion, vibration mounts | Oil, fuel, sunlight |
| SBR | Low-cost static seals in water | Oil and fuel |

If you are not sure, send us the medium, the working temperature and the
pressure, and we will recommend the compound for the part.
