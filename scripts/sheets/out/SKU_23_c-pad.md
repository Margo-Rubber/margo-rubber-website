## 03 Rubber compound options [🛑 The load-pad compound is missing]
> live: LIVE → EPDM (SELECTED), NBR, Neoprene, SBR — natural rubber absent on a load-bearing pad
- **Wrong default, and a missing option**:
  > 🛑 Natural rubber is not on the menu, and it has the best fatigue resistance and highest resilience of the eight Margo mixes — which is what a pad that is loaded and unloaded for years is judged on.
  > 🛑 EPDM is pre-selected. It weathers well and creeps more than natural rubber under sustained load, and on a pad whose job is to hold a gap, creep is the failure. The default should follow the duty rather than the weather.
  > Margo produces every part in every compound it mixes (client-instructed 2026-09-02). Nothing is removed — all eight are ranked, and the four missing from the live selector are added.
- **Selector** (Rank | Compound | Temperature | Why, for a slotted packing pad):
  | Recommended | Natural rubber | −20 to +90 °C | Best fatigue resistance and highest resilience of the eight, and the least creep under sustained load — so the gap it was fitted to hold stays held. Not for oil, and it perishes in prolonged sunlight |
  | Recommended | Neoprene | −40 to +120 °C | Where the pad is exposed to weather or sees oil. Good fatigue life with better ozone resistance than natural rubber |
  | Suitable | SBR | −30 to +100 °C | Low-cost enclosed packing. Fatigue life below natural rubber and no oil resistance |
  | Suitable | EPDM | −40 to +140 °C | Weather, ozone and steam. Creeps more than natural rubber, so allow for it in the deflection |
  | Suitable | NBR | −30 to +120 °C | Where the pad sits in oil. Poor ozone resistance, so not for an exposed position |
  | Available | Silicone | −60 to +220 °C | High temperature. Low tear strength makes it a weak choice for a slotted pad, where the slot is a stress raiser |
  | 🛑 Not recommended | FKM · AEM | See the compound table | Expensive sealing compounds with no fatigue advantage in a packing pad |
  _Live set: EPDM (SELECTED), NBR, Neoprene, SBR — four of eight, unranked, and the standard answer is not among them.
The silicone note is specific to this shape. A slot is a stress raiser and tearing starts at its root, so the compound with the lowest tear strength is a worse choice here than it would be on a closed pad_

## 06 From compound to certification [The slot changes tooling and inspection]

  | 02 | Mould design and CNC tool manufacture, in-house, held as a controlled record. Outline and thickness are set together to reach the load and deflection, and the slot root is radiused rather than square, because a sharp corner is where a slotted pad tears. |
