## 03 Rubber compound options [🛑 SBR cracks from ozone · natural rubber missing]
> live: LIVE → Neoprene, SBR — two of the eight, presented as equals · natural rubber, the impact compound, is not offered
- **All eight, always — and the real trade-off on this part**:
  > Margo produces every part in every compound it mixes (client-instructed 2026-09-02). The live selector offers two, understating the offer by six.
  > On a bump stop the ranking is a genuine engineering trade-off, not a single right answer — and that is what makes this section worth writing properly. Natural rubber has the best rebound and fatigue life of the eight, which is what an impact part wants. But the documented failure mode of a solid rubber stop is ozone cracking, and natural rubber is poor under ozone and UV.
  > So the answer depends on where the stop sits: sheltered — inside a door shut, under a bonnet, in a console — natural rubber wins on life. Exposed to daylight, EPDM wins, because a stop that cracks has failed regardless of how well it absorbed impact.
  > 🛑 SBR, one of the two compounds currently offered, is poor on both counts — less rebound than natural rubber and poor ozone resistance. It is the cheapest option and the page presents it as an equal.
- **Selector** (Rank | Compound | Temperature | Hardness | For this part):
  | RECOMMENDED — sheltered | Natural rubber | −20 to +90 °C | 30–80 Shore A | Best rebound and fatigue life of the eight — the impact compound. For door shuts, bonnet underside, consoles and enclosed linkages |
  | RECOMMENDED — exposed | EPDM | −40 to +140 °C | 40–90 Shore A | Ozone and UV resistant. For any stop that sees daylight, where cracking beats fatigue as the failure mode |
  | Suitable | Neoprene | −40 to +120 °C | 40–90 Shore A | The compromise — good weathering, moderate rebound. Specify where the stop also sees oil |
  | Available | NBR | −30 to +120 °C | 40–90 Shore A | Specify where the stop sees oil or fuel — an engine-bay linkage stop |
  | Available | AEM | −40 to +175 °C

  | 🛑 Not recommended | SBR | −30 to +100 °C | 40–90 Shore A | Poor ozone resistance and less rebound than natural rubber — worse than both alternatives on the two properties that matter. Currently offered on this page |
