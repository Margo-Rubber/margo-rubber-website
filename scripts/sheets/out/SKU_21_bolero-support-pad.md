## 03 Rubber compound options [🛑 A fixed part should not offer a compound menu]
> live: LIVE → EPDM (SELECTED), NBR, Neoprene, SBR — natural rubber absent, on a body mount
- **Two faults, one structural**:
  > 🛑 Natural rubber is not on the menu, and it is what body mounts are made from — best fatigue resistance and highest resilience of the eight Margo mixes. A mount is a fatigue part before it is anything else.
  > 🛑 And the block should not be a menu at all here. On a bespoke part the buyer picks the compound; on a fitment part Margo has already picked it, because the pad has to match the original in stiffness and life. Offering four compounds invites a distributor to order a body mount in SBR.
  > Reframe it as “supplied in” with alternatives on request. Margo produces every part in every compound it mixes (client-instructed 2026-09-02), so nothing is removed — all eight remain available and are ranked, with the standard clearly marked.
- **Selector** (Rank | Compound | Temperature | Why, for a body mount):
  | Supplied as standard | Natural rubber | −20 to +90 °C | What body mounts are made from. Best fatigue resistance and highest resilience of the eight, so it survives a decade of loading and returns to height. Not for oil, and it perishes in prolonged sunlight |
  | Alternative | Neoprene | −40 to +120 °C | Where the mount is exposed or sees oil. Good fatigue life with better ozone resistance than natural rubber — the usual choice for an underbody position |
  | On request | SBR | −30 to +100 °C | Lower cost, and fatigue life below natural rubber. Not recommended for a load-carrying mount |
  | On request | EPDM | −40 to +140 °C | Weather and ozone resistance, and it creeps more than natural rubber under sustained load |
  | On request | NBR | −30 to +120 °C | Where the mount sits in oil. Poor ozone resistance, so not for an exposed position |
