## 03 Rubber compound options [🛑 The load-pad compound is missing from the menu]
> live: LIVE → EPDM (SELECTED), NBR, Neoprene, SBR — natural rubber absent
- **Wrong default, and a missing option**:
  > 🛑 Natural rubber is not offered, and it is the compound engine mounts, body mounts and machine mounts are made from. Best fatigue resistance and highest resilience of the eight Margo mixes — and a pad that is loaded and unloaded for a decade is a fatigue problem before it is anything else.
  > 🛑 EPDM is pre-selected. It weathers well and it creeps more than natural rubber under sustained load. On a support pad the default should follow the duty rather than the weather.
  > Margo produces every part in every compound it mixes (client-instructed 2026-09-02). Nothing is removed — all eight are ranked, and the four missing from the live selector are added.
- **Selector** (Rank | Compound | Temperature | Why, for a load-bearing pad):
  | Recommended | Natural rubber | −20 to +90 °C | The classic mounting compound. Best fatigue resistance and highest resilience of the eight, so it survives repeated loading and returns to height. Not for oil, and it perishes in prolonged sunlight |
  | Recommended | Neoprene | −40 to +120 °C | Where the pad is exposed to weather or sees oil. Good fatigue life, moderate oil resistance, better ozone resistance than natural rubber. The usual choice under a vehicle |
  | Suitable | SBR | −30 to +100 °C | Low-cost indoor and enclosed load pads. Fatigue life below natural rubber and no oil resistance |
  | Suitable | EPDM | −40 to +140 °C | Weather, ozone and steam. Creeps more than natural rubber under sustained load, so allow for it in the deflection |
  | Suitable | NBR | −30 to +120 °C | Where the pad sits in oil. Poor ozone resistance, so not for an exposed position |
  | Available | Silicone | −60 to +220 °C | High temperature. Low tear strength and poor fatigue resistance make it a weak choice under load |
  | 🛑 Not recommended | FKM | −20 to +250 °C
continuous to +200 °C | 2–3× the cost and no fatigue advantage. Specify only where the medium demands it |
  | 🛑 Not recommended | AEM | −40 to +175 °C
continuous to +160 °C | A hot-oil sealing compound. Nothing it offers is useful in a load pad |
  _Live set: EPDM (SELECTED), NBR, Neoprene, SBR — four of eight, unranked, and the industry-standard answer is not among them.
The ranking is driven by fatigue life rather than chemical resistance, which is the opposite of every other compound block in this set and correct for a part that works mechanically rather than by sealing.
Natural rubber’s weaknesses are stated in the same cell as its strength, so the recommendation does not need a caveat elsewhere_
