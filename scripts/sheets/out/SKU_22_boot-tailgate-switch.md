## 03 Rubber compound options [Right default · two options will perish]
> live: LIVE → EPDM (SELECTED), NBR, Neoprene, SBR — correct default, two compounds that crack outdoors
- **The default is right; the menu is not**:
  > EPDM is pre-selected and it is correct for a component that lives on the exterior of a vehicle — ozone, UV and water resistance, and it stays flexible at −40 °C, which is exactly when the button has to still work. Do not let the developer change it.
  > 🛑 NBR and SBR both crack in ozone and UV, and this part is mounted outdoors. They are also the two cheapest on the menu, offered without a word of caution — so a buyer choosing on price gets a boot that hardens and splits, and then water reaches the switch.
  > Margo produces every part in every compound it mixes (client-instructed 2026-09-02). Nothing is removed — all eight are ranked, and the two unsuitable ones carry the reason.
- **Selector** (Rank | Compound | Temperature | Why, for an exposed switch boot):
  | Recommended | EPDM | −40 to +140 °C | The correct answer and correctly pre-selected. Ozone, UV and water resistance, and it stays soft at −40 °C — which is when the button most needs to work |
  | Recommended | Silicone | −60 to +220 °C | The best cold flexibility of the eight, so the lightest and most consistent press in winter. Tears more easily, which matters on a thin diaphragm |
  | Suitable | Neoprene | −40 to +120 °C | Reasonable weathering and ozone resistance. Stiffer cold than EPDM, so the button gets heavier in winter |
  | 🛑 Not recommended | NBR | −30 to +120 °C | Cracks in ozone and UV. Offered on the live page; on an exterior switch it perishes and lets water through |
  | 🛑 Not recommended | SBR | −30 to +100 °C | Also poor in ozone and UV, and no better cold. The cheapest option on the live menu and the shortest-lived |
  | Available | Natural rubber | −20 to +90 °C | Excellent flexibility and tear strength, but it perishes outdoors faster than any of the above |
  | 🛑 Not recommended | FKM · AEM | See the compound table | Stiff and expensive, and neither brings anything a pressed boot needs |
  _Live set: EPDM (SELECTED), NBR, Neoprene, SBR — and half of it perishes on an exterior part.
Silicone is promoted here on an argument that applies to almost nothing else in the catalogue. Its weakness is tear strength and its strength is cold flexibility — and on a part whose failure mode is “the button is stiff in winter”, that trade is worth making_
