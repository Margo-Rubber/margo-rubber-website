## 03 Rubber compound options [Right default · two options will perish, and the sponge case is missing]
> live: LIVE → EPDM (SELECTED), NBR, Neoprene, SBR — correct default, no sponge grade
- **The default is right; the menu is not**:
  > EPDM is pre-selected and it is correct. The flap sits at the front of the engine bay in direct airflow — road spray, sunlight through the grille, ozone and radiator heat — and EPDM handles all four while staying flexible at −40 °C. Do not let the developer change it.
  > 🛑 NBR and SBR both crack in ozone and UV, and this part is in the airstream behind an open grille. They are the two cheapest on the menu and neither carries a warning.
  > 🛑 And the sponge option is absent entirely — the form most likely to seal a variable gap. Sponge is not a compound but a form, and it needs to appear alongside the compounds rather than instead of them.
  > Margo produces every part in every compound it mixes (client-instructed 2026-09-02). Nothing is removed — all eight are ranked, with the sponge form shown as its own choice.
- **Selector** (Rank | Compound or form | Temperature | Why, for a shroud flap):
  | Recommended | EPDM closed-cell sponge | −40 to +100 °C | Fills a gap that varies along its length at almost no closing force, which is what this seal has to do. Specified on density and compression force deflection, to an ASTM D1056 grade |
  | Recommended | EPDM solid, 40–55 Shore A | −40 to +140 °C | Where the gap is consistent and a tougher flap is wanted. Correctly pre-selected on the live page, though at the wrong hardness |
  | Suitable | Neoprene | −40 to +120 °C | Reasonable weathering and ozone resistance, with some oil resistance if the flap sits where oil mist reaches it |
  | Suitable | Silicone | −60 to +220 °C | Where the flap runs close to a heat source. Tears more easily, which matters on a thin flexible strip |
  | 🛑 Not recommended | NBR | −30 to +120 °C | Cracks in ozone and UV, and this part sits in the airstream behind an open grille |
  | 🛑 Not recommended | SBR | −30 to +100 °C | Also poor in ozone and UV. The cheapest option on the live menu and the shortest-lived here |
  | Available | Natural rubber | −20 to +90 °C | Excellent flexibility and tear strength, but it perishes in sunlight faster than any of the above |
  | 🛑 Not recommended | FKM · AEM | See the compound table | Expensive sealing compounds. Neither brings anything a shroud flap needs |
  _🛑 Live set: EPDM (SELECTED), NBR, Neoprene, SBR — and half of it perishes in UV on a part behind a grille.
Sponge leads over solid, which reverses the usual order in this catalogue and is right here: the gap varies, and conforming matters more than toughness.
The closing line puts the form decision ahead of the compound decision, which is the correct sequence and the opposite of how the live selector presents it_
